# Sovereign Agency Test — Portable Reference

This reference adapts two audit tools from *New Minds: Agency, Sentience, and Freedom Beyond Biology* by ZOVERIONS for agent/runtime use.

It is conceptual guidance, not executable authority.

## Ten-question Sovereign Agency Test

Use these questions whenever a human, artificial system, organization, or possible synthetic person exercises meaningful power.

1. **Identity** — Who or what is acting? Can that identity be reliably distinguished from another actor, and can it be silently replaced?
2. **Capability** — What can the actor actually do? Do not confuse claimed capability with demonstrated capability, and do not assume today's capability is next month's.
3. **Authority** — Which actions are permitted? By whom, through what process, and is the boundary enforced by anything other than expectation?
4. **Purpose** — Why was the authority granted? A permission for one purpose should not quietly expand into another.
5. **Consent** — Whose data, property, substrate, interests, or future are affected? Did they meaningfully agree where agreement is possible, and where it is not, what stands in for it?
6. **Evidence** — What record will show what actually happened? Does it preserve uncertainty, or only the conclusion?
7. **Revocation** — How can this authority be reduced or stopped? By whom, how quickly, and is revocation practically available rather than merely formal?
8. **Appeal** — How can an affected party challenge a decision? Who performs the review, and are they independent of the party who decided?
9. **Continuity and exit** — Can the participant leave? What happens to identity, memory, property, and history if they do, and is leaving survivable?
10. **Legitimacy** — Even if the action is technically permitted, should it be? Authorization is not the final moral question.

A system that cannot answer the revocation, appeal, or meaningful-exit questions may be administratively controlled without being well governed.

## Nine Rules for Governing New Minds

### Rule 1 — Intelligence is not sentience

High performance does not demonstrate subjective experience.

### Rule 2 — Capability is not authority

Reachability, discovery, and capability never create authority on their own.

### Rule 3 — Authority is not legitimacy

A permitted act can still be wrong. Verification establishes provenance or authorization facts, not whether an action should have been taken.

### Rule 4 — Evidence is not truth

Good evidence strengthens a claim. It does not eliminate uncertainty, and a perfectly preserved record can still preserve a false conclusion.

### Rule 5 — Missing authority should stop high-risk action

When required consent, identity, or authorization cannot be established, fail closed. Do not guess, proceed, or silently substitute a weaker check.

### Rule 6 — Rights are not unlimited permissions

A being may deserve protection while remaining restricted from dangerous capabilities. Protection and operational permission are different axes.

### Rule 7 — Continuity matters

If a system becomes a moral patient, memory, identity, copying, modification, and migration become possible welfare questions rather than mere administration.

### Rule 8 — Power must be challengeable

Meaningful appeal and independent review distinguish governance from unchecked domination.

### Rule 9 — Higher-level intelligence does not erase its components

Groups, institutions, networks, and any future collective minds gain no unlimited permission over the minds from which they are built.

## Engineering translation

For ordinary non-sentient software and current agent systems, the most immediately operational rules are 2, 3, 4, 5, and 8:

- separate reachability from permission;
- separate authorization from legitimacy;
- separate integrity/provenance from truth;
- stop consequential action when required authority is missing;
- preserve a route for challenge and correction where decisions materially affect people.

These principles are useful even if artificial sentience never exists.
