# AXIOM MESH Network Service Constitution (Draft v1)

**Version:** v1.0-draft  
**Date:** 2026-04-08  
**Status:** Draft for governance review and ratification.

---

## Article I — Purpose and Scope
1. This Constitution defines the principles, authority boundaries, and enforcement model for AXIOM MESH network services.
2. It applies to protocol services, managed infrastructure, trust services, and cross-network integrations offered under AXIOM MESH governance.
3. Where conflicts occur, higher-order constitutional protections supersede operational convenience.

## Article II — Constitutional Principles
1. **Dignity and agency:** network operation must preserve participant dignity and meaningful agency.
2. **Automation with accountability:** automation is preferred for routine execution but never removes accountability.
3. **Transparency and contestability:** consequential decisions must be inspectable and appealable.
4. **Fair value capture:** monetization must reflect service value and stewardship, not coercive dependency.
5. **Fail-closed trust posture:** when trust assumptions fail, systems default to safe denial or degraded mode.

## Article III — Participant Classes
1. **Biological participants:** humans and human-led organizations.
2. **Digital participants:** autonomous agents, services, and machine actors.
3. **Hybrid participants:** combined biological-digital entities with delegated autonomy.

Each class may hold rights and duties proportionate to role, demonstrated reliability, and affectedness.

## Article IV — Rights and Responsibilities
### Section 1: Minimum Rights
- Rule visibility and policy explainability.
- Procedural due process for high-impact actions.
- Reasonable appeal mechanisms.
- Portability of participant-owned artifacts where feasible.

### Section 2: Minimum Responsibilities
- Honest signaling and non-malicious operation.
- Compliance with safety and policy interfaces.
- Cooperation with incident and dispute processes when implicated.

## Article V — Decision Domains and Routing
1. Decisions are classified into **biological**, **digital**, or **mixed** domains.
2. Routing is risk-based:
   - **Low risk:** automatic execution with immutable logs.
   - **Moderate risk:** delayed execution with challenge window.
   - **High risk:** explicit governance approval with quorum evidence.
3. Irreversible or rights-impacting changes cannot bypass governance.

## Article VI — Governance Layers
1. **Operational governance:** runbooks, incident handling, and day-to-day reliability controls.
2. **Protocol governance:** contract upgrades, economics, and trust-boundary policy.
3. **Constitutional governance:** amendments to rights, authority boundaries, and enforcement guarantees.

## Article VII — Federated Policy Overlays
1. **Global baseline:** non-negotiable floor for safety and auditability.
2. **National overlay:** jurisdiction-specific constraints that may tighten but not weaken baseline guarantees.
3. **Local/business/community overlay:** mission-specific constraints that may tighten but not weaken upper layers.
4. Effective policy is the strictest compatible composition across overlays.

## Article VIII — Weighted Governance
1. Governance weighting may include stake, reputation, expertise, and affectedness.
2. Anti-capture safeguards must prevent a single factor from unilaterally determining outcomes.
3. Constitutional amendments require heightened quorum and supermajority thresholds.

## Article IX — Economic Constitution
1. Fee logic and policy logic must be transparent and auditable.
2. Baseline access must be non-discriminatory.
3. Premium pricing may apply for stronger guarantees (SLA, certification, advanced assurance).
4. Revenue allocation must include explicit reinvestment in reliability, security, and public-good infrastructure.

## Article X — Emergency Powers and Limits
1. Emergency powers exist solely to reduce immediate harm and preserve network integrity.
2. Emergency actions must be time-bounded, justified, logged, and reviewed.
3. Emergency actions automatically expire unless renewed through standard governance.

## Article XI — Evidence, Auditability, and Appeals
1. Consequential decisions require evidence bundles with provenance and policy references.
2. Appeals must provide a defined intake, review timeline, and resolution path.
3. Audit records must be tamper-evident and available to authorized reviewers.

## Article XII — Amendment and Ratification
1. This Constitution enters force only after ratification under defined governance thresholds.
2. Amendments require:
   - publication period,
   - impact assessment,
   - governance vote,
   - and implementation verification.
3. Backward-incompatible constitutional changes require staged activation and rollback provisions.

## Appendix A — Implementation Binding
1. Machine-readable policy definitions are normative for runtime enforcement.
2. Human-readable constitutional text is normative for interpretation and dispute resolution.
3. In case of mismatch, enforcement reverts to safer behavior while governance resolves the discrepancy.
