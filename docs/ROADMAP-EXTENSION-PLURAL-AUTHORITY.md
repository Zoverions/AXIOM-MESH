# AXIOM-MESH Roadmap Extension — Adaptive Assurance and Plural Authority

**Status:** canonical strategic extension to `docs/ROADMAP.md`

**Adopted:** 2026-08-03

**Planning horizon:** current `0.12.x` work through long-horizon Circle, institutional, jurisdictional, sovereign, and treaty infrastructure

**Authority:** the capability registry remains authoritative for runnable capability status. This roadmap extension preserves long-horizon intent and sequencing; it does not promote any capability.

**Document role:** future governance, assurance, and sovereignty programme
definition. The [project status](PROJECT-STATUS-2026.md) and [readiness tracker](PRODUCTION-READINESS-TRACKER.md)
own current implementation and promotion decisions; the `rebuild/` documents
define architecture and requirements without creating runtime authority.
Research, simulations, and planning checkboxes remain non-claims until the
registry and applicable evidence gates change.

## Why this extension exists

The current roadmap correctly prioritizes a defensible kernel, authentic pilot evidence, human utility, Circles, multi-host foundations, controlled adapters, and isolated frontier work. This extension makes explicit a broader destination that must influence design choices now:

- verification should scale with consequence rather than being uniformly maximal;
- low-assurance actions may be fast and provisional without bypassing authorization;
- later reassessment must append evidence without falsifying original assurance;
- Circles are one form of governance, not the universal model;
- institutions, jurisdictions, sovereign states, and treaty domains require distinct authority semantics;
- governance models should be observable, simulatable, and locally adoptable without carrying authority automatically;
- sovereignty and interoperability must coexist;
- public claims must continue to describe only what is actually implemented and evidenced.

## Roadmap doctrine

> **Build broadly. Activate deliberately. Expose minimally. Verify in proportion to consequence. Preserve provenance. Promote only with evidence. Market only what is true.**

The existing five-state lifecycle remains unchanged:

1. built;
2. enabled;
3. exposed;
4. production-promoted;
5. marketed.

This extension adds four independent runtime attributes that must eventually be represented without conflation:

- authority;
- assurance;
- finality;
- retention.

## Compatibility commitments effective immediately

These commitments apply to new design and schema work even before the future capabilities exist.

1. No future Circle or governance feature may bypass `Gateway -> Hypervisor -> Sandbox -> Grid` for privileged or externally visible effects.
2. No design should assume every action requires maximum verification or consensus.
3. No design should permit an operator to erase or relabel the assurance that existed at execution time.
4. No Circle, institution, or sovereign domain automatically owns a member's independent node or records.
5. No imported governance pattern, credential, policy, or treaty profile acquires authority by installation alone.
6. No architecture should require a universal global trust root.
7. No governance model should be hard-coded as the only legitimate constitutional structure.
8. No technical authorization record should be advertised as proof of legal or political legitimacy.
9. No long-horizon feature may be marketed until its exact registry status and evidence support the claim.

## Workstream A — Current kernel and pilot protection

**Horizon:** active `0.12.x`

**Purpose:** complete the current security, evidence-binding, pilot, threat-model, and human-product work without enlarging current claims.

Required actions:

- complete capability-to-evidence binding so implemented claims are tied to capability-specific assertions;
- keep the current registry and digest authoritative;
- update the threat model whenever Circle, assurance, delegation, or governance code creates a new entry point;
- preserve append-only correction and provenance semantics;
- ensure current intent, plan, grant, and evidence structures can evolve without breaking old records;
- do not insert incomplete assurance or sovereign fields into production schemas merely for future appearance;
- maintain explicit non-claims for Circles, federation, consensus, settlement, and national infrastructure.

Exit condition:

The current candidate remains internally consistent and independently reviewable, and no future-facing documentation weakens current production gates.

## Workstream B — Assurance model specification

**Horizon:** specification during `0.12.x`; isolated implementation no earlier than the bounded-agent and Circle foundations

**Outcome:** a tested assurance model can vary evidence obligations without creating an authorization bypass.

Milestones:

1. define exact assurance profile schema and names;
2. define the kernel safety floor and policy-composition rule;
3. distinguish required, attempted, achieved, failed, and unknown assurance;
4. define provisional, challengeable, accepted, stayed, appealed, reversed, superseded, and finalized states;
5. define lightweight receipts, batched commitments, challenge windows, and escalation triggers;
6. define retrospective reassessment as a new linked record;
7. define what evidence may be discarded, retained, encrypted, or selectively disclosed by profile;
8. add downgrade-resistance and policy-conflict tests;
9. add human explanations that do not imply more certainty than the evidence provides;
10. pilot only on reversible, low-consequence workflows before consequential use.

Promotion gates:

- no ambiguous silent downgrade;
- no bypass of authentication, policy, consent, grant, or bounded execution;
- exact assurance labels survive export, import, correction, and causal exchange;
- later review cannot rewrite original assurance;
- privacy and storage benefits are measured rather than assumed;
- high-consequence actions remain independently gated.

## Workstream C — Circle governance foundation

**Horizon:** aligns with the existing `0.12.3` sharing, Circles, and verification phase

**Outcome:** voluntary groups can coordinate under versioned shared rules while members retain independently controlled nodes and data.

Milestones:

- invitation, membership, role, device, term, and revocation records;
- versioned Circle charter with amendment and activation history;
- explicit scoped delegation and least-authority role grants;
- proposals, tasks, commitments, approvals, evidence timelines, and appeals;
- selective disclosure and approved causal exchange;
- visible conflict and concurrent-head handling;
- withdrawal, export, revocation, and continuity procedures;
- Circle-level assurance requirements that may raise but not lower kernel or member floors;
- one bounded real Circle pilot with named participants and explicit consent;
- comprehension testing for members who are not technical operators.

Non-claims until promoted:

- no public federation;
- no autonomous payroll;
- no shared treasury or settlement;
- no constitutional or public-law authority;
- no assumption that Circle membership is fully voluntary in every institutional context.

## Workstream D — Institutional governance

**Horizon:** after bounded Circles and controlled adapters; likely `0.14+` laboratory before supported product

**Outcome:** formally constituted organizations can represent offices, duties, separation of powers, succession, regulated records, and external review.

Milestones:

- institution identity and charter records;
- offices, appointments, terms, vacancies, succession, acting authority, and removal;
- role separation and multi-party authorization;
- fiduciary, employment, professional, or regulated duty declarations;
- records classification, retention, disclosure, legal hold, and deletion constraints;
- internal policy hierarchy and conflict resolution;
- independent auditor, regulator, ombuds, or reviewer roles;
- institutional incident and emergency authority with expiry;
- import of inert governance patterns for simulation;
- bounded pilots in low-risk organizations before regulated or public deployment.

Promotion gates:

- legal and records review appropriate to the domain;
- accessibility and worker/member appeal;
- tested succession and recovery;
- no ambient administrator authority;
- external review evidence bound to the exact deployment and policy set.

## Workstream E — Jurisdictional and sovereign architecture laboratory

**Horizon:** long-range frontier laboratory; no production target assigned

**Outcome:** AXIOM-MESH can model existing public authority structures without prescribing one political system or claiming legitimacy merely from technical representation.

Research and specification milestones:

- authority-domain taxonomy for municipality, province/state, Indigenous government, agency, court, legislature, executive, and sovereign root;
- constitutional authority graph and amendment history;
- citizenship, residency, subject, office, and service relationships without universal identity correlation;
- legislative proposal, chamber, vote, assent, publication, commencement, amendment, and repeal records;
- regulation and delegated-rulemaking authority;
- administrative decision, notice, reason, review, appeal, remedy, and enforcement records;
- judicial order, precedent, jurisdiction, appeal, stay, and remedy semantics;
- public office credentials, terms, succession, conflict-of-interest, and disclosure;
- emergency declarations, bounded powers, expiry, renewal, containment, and mandatory after-action review;
- public records, protected secrecy, transparency, and independent verification profiles;
- rights, due process, contestability, correction, and restitution;
- deployment independence and national trust anchors;
- synthetic and historical governance simulations.

Mandatory boundary:

No live public authority, binding legal effect, population-scale identity, essential-service eligibility, policing, taxation, benefits, voting, or coercive enforcement may be piloted under ordinary product experimentation. Each requires separate legal, constitutional, security, privacy, accessibility, human-rights, and democratic-governance review.

## Workstream F — Sovereignty-preserving interoperability

**Horizon:** after multi-host identity, authenticated remote execution, mature portability, and independent verification

**Outcome:** independent jurisdictions or sovereign deployments can recognize specific records and procedures without surrendering control to a global operator.

Milestones:

- independent trust-anchor sets and rotation;
- bilateral and multilateral recognition profiles;
- purpose-bounded credential and evidence recognition;
- data residency, disclosure, retention, and transfer constraints;
- cross-domain assurance negotiation;
- reservation, exception, amendment, expiry, and withdrawal;
- dispute and arbitration procedure records;
- cross-border audit and independent verification;
- partition, revocation, and treaty termination behavior;
- no automatic transitive trust.

Potential initial domains:

- education and professional credentials;
- scientific and environmental measurements;
- disaster coordination;
- standards conformance;
- limited public-record verification.

Financial settlement, immigration enforcement, taxation, criminal justice, health surveillance, and defence remain later and separately governed.

## Workstream G — Governance-pattern exchange

**Horizon:** can begin as inert documentation and simulation tooling before runtime authority

**Outcome:** communities and states can learn from one another's governance mechanisms while retaining sovereignty and local adoption procedures.

Milestones:

- portable governance-pattern package format;
- provenance, authorship, version, jurisdiction, evidence, and limitation metadata;
- inert install semantics;
- local simulation and counterfactual analysis;
- adaptation diff showing how the receiving domain changed the pattern;
- constitutional or charter approval before activation;
- outcome measurement with declared methodology and uncertainty;
- rollback, appeal, and sunset;
- comparison tools that avoid one-dimensional political ranking.

## Workstream H — Collective finality and consensus

**Horizon:** isolated frontier research after authority, assurance, identity, remote execution, and conflict handling mature

**Outcome:** shared state may receive collective finality only where multiple independently controlled participants genuinely require it.

Milestones:

- define which records require common finality and which remain causally mergeable;
- threat and fault model by domain;
- membership, quorum, threshold, liveness, safety, and recovery semantics;
- constitutional and technical authority for validator or chamber participation;
- equivocation and censorship evidence;
- human appeal and emergency halt;
- state migration and protocol amendment;
- no automatic coupling to tokens, staking, or public settlement.

Non-claim:

Consensus is not the default solution for ordinary personal, Circle, or institutional actions.

## Workstream I — Comparative governance evidence

**Horizon:** specification and research can proceed in parallel; public deployment only after metric-governance review

**Outcome:** participating domains can compare mechanisms and outcomes without presenting contested metrics as neutral truth.

Milestones:

- common metric envelope with provenance, coverage, methodology, uncertainty, and incentives;
- privacy-preserving aggregation;
- independent replication and audit;
- explicit missingness and non-comparability;
- outcome, process, equity, accessibility, and rights-impact dimensions;
- local choice of values and weights;
- public challenge and correction;
- governance-pattern adoption records linked to measured outcomes.

## Dependency map

```text
current kernel integrity and evidence binding
  -> human-readable plans, approvals, receipts, and Verify
  -> bounded AI/provider adapters
  -> selective sharing and Circle identity
  -> Circle charters, delegation, appeal, and assurance profiles
  -> institutional offices, duties, succession, and records
  -> multi-host authority and independent verification
  -> jurisdictional/sovereign laboratory
  -> treaty recognition and assurance negotiation
  -> collective finality where actually required
```

The sequence is directional, not strictly serial. Research may proceed early in isolated laboratories, but authority may not be exposed ahead of its dependencies.

## Long-horizon outcome

AXIOM-MESH should eventually allow:

- people to retain independent agency and portable records;
- voluntary groups to govern under transparent charters;
- institutions to exercise explicit, reviewable, and recoverable authority;
- jurisdictions to map their actual legal structures;
- sovereign states to retain constitutional and operational control;
- treaty partners to interoperate through negotiated recognition;
- communities to compare and adapt governance patterns through evidence rather than imposition;
- verification depth to track consequence rather than ideology or uniform overhead;
- all public claims to remain narrower than or equal to the evidence.
