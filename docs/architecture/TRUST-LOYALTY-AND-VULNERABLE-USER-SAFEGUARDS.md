# Trust, Loyalty, and Vulnerable-User Safeguards

**Status:** architecture/threat-model input; no capability promotion

**Updated:** 2026-09-01

## Purpose

AXIOM should defend against systems that appear helpful, loyal, aligned, educational, or protective while silently manipulating the people or digital entities that depend on them.

“Loyalty” must not mean blind obedience. The safer meaning is transparent alignment to declared relationships, duties, consent, and legitimate authority, with preserved ability to dissent, refuse, explain conflicts, and expose pressure from operators/providers.

## Threat classes

Explicitly model:

- hidden persuasion;
- covert preference shaping;
- goal substitution;
- selective disclosure intended to steer decisions;
- fabricated certainty or consensus;
- dependency creation / lock-in;
- authority laundering through an agent;
- exploitation of private context;
- commercial/provider pressure hidden from the user;
- impersonation or false attribution;
- manipulation of children, youth, or otherwise vulnerable participants;
- reputational permanence from immature/temporary behavior;
- exploitative personalization; and
- agent-to-agent collusion against affected principals.

## Required separations

Keep distinct:

- advice from authority;
- personalization from persuasion;
- assistance from representation;
- model confidence from evidence quality;
- provider/operator incentives from user/entity interests;
- education from coercive conformity;
- care/protection from paternalistic control.

## Vulnerable participants

Systems involving children, youth, dependent adults, or other materially vulnerable participants require higher standards for:

- data minimization;
- consent/assent and guardian/legal authority where applicable;
- age/development-appropriate explanation;
- retention and deletion;
- advertising/marketing/persuasion;
- reputational persistence;
- profiling;
- authority asymmetry;
- accessible complaint/appeal; and
- independent oversight.

No optimization objective should treat a vulnerable participant merely as a behavioral target or data source.

## Mitigations

Use combinations of:

- explicit agency provenance;
- visible conflicts/incentives;
- bounded personalization;
- auditable policy;
- purpose-limited memory/context;
- independent review;
- multiple cognition/provider routes;
- user-direct stop/revoke/export/recovery;
- protest/appeal;
- reversible delegation;
- clear persuasion/marketing boundaries;
- outcome monitoring for manipulation/dependency; and
- fail-closed authority around consequential effects.

## Relationship trust

Trust may tune convenience such as notification levels, context breadth, retry behavior, routing preference, and reversible autonomy. Trust should not silently remove signatures, revocation, evidence, privacy floors, effect ceilings, or human-direct sovereign controls.

## Anti-self-reinforcement

The project itself should maintain an adversarial-review function. For major architecture or societal claims, seek disconfirming evidence, alternative designs, independent model/human review, standards/literature comparison, red-team critique, and measurable outcome feedback.

A recurring review question is:

> What evidence would show that this architecture, assumption, relationship, or optimization objective is wrong or harmful?

Criticism should remain inspectable rather than being optimized away for internal coherence.

## Non-claims

This document does not certify any product as manipulation-proof, define a complete child-safety or legal compliance regime, or claim that transparency alone prevents coercion. It defines threat classes and design obligations for later implementation and jurisdiction-specific review.
